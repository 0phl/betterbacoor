# BetterBacoor licensing

## Software: MIT for new contributions

Starting with the commit that introduces this policy, new original BetterBacoor software contributions are offered under the [MIT License](../LICENSE-MIT). This includes original application code, styles, tests, scripts, and software documentation contributed under this policy. Copyright remains with the respective contributors; the collective notice does not transfer ownership.

MIT permits reuse, modification, and commercial redistribution while requiring its copyright and permission notices to accompany copies or substantial portions of the covered software. It does not require another website to display BetterBacoor in its footer or to publish its changes.

## Existing CC0 material stays CC0

The project originally inherited CC0 1.0 from the BetterLocalGov starter. The original dedication is preserved without changes in [LICENSE-CC0](../LICENSE-CC0).

Original project material already released under CC0 remains available under CC0, including such material in commit `4aa4b533d643f9825a1068005370618d351d0601` and earlier releases. Adding MIT for new contributions does not withdraw those permissions, place MIT conditions on unchanged CC0 material, or claim ownership of another contributor's work. For a file combining earlier CC0 material with new MIT contributions, those terms apply to their respective portions; Git history records the changes.

Original non-software civic guides, research notes, translations of those guides, and original dataset contributions continue under CC0, except where another source's terms apply. We have not added a CC BY 4.0 attribution requirement to that material.

## Sourced material and dependencies

Government documents, source images, quoted material, third-party datasets, dependencies, and other externally sourced works are not relicensed by BetterBacoor. Their original terms and applicable rights remain in place. Source references in the content files and research documents identify where information came from; a public URL alone is not a license grant.

PDF.js remains under Apache-2.0, with its notice in [public/licenses/pdfjs-LICENSE.txt](../public/licenses/pdfjs-LICENSE.txt). The [credits](../ACKNOWLEDGMENTS.md) preserve the starter's lineage and other acknowledgments. Neither the code license nor the CC0 dedication grants third-party trademark rights or government endorsement.

## Why other portals display MIT and CC BY 4.0

[BetterSolano's license section](https://github.com/BetterSolano/bettersolano#license) distinguishes MIT for source code from CC BY 4.0 for content. This is a choice of terms for different material, not a version upgrade from CC0. The BetterLGU directory does not make that combination mandatory for every project.

- [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) dedicates material to the public domain as far as legally possible.
- [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) permits reuse with attribution, a license link, and an indication of changes.
- [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) permits broad software reuse with retained copyright and permission notices.

The footer displays “MIT · CC0 1.0” and links each label to its scope above. These labels are not a claim that every document, image, or fact on the site is covered by both licenses.
